# Nebula — 30-day first-revenue plan

30 September–30 October 2026. Working plan, not a forecast or a promise of sales.

## The decision

Run a **bounded sales test of the complete bouquet builder**. The commercial job
is to learn whether an independent florist will pay $29/month for a shop-specific
link that lets a customer compose a visual request and send it to the florist's
existing WhatsApp conversation. The florist still approves stock and final price.
There are currently **zero paying shops, zero price agreements and zero real
customer requests**. Do not treat a polished demo, followers or competitor claims
as evidence of demand.

Execution assumption: reserve roughly 60–90 minutes on each outreach day plus
one focused site-update day and one content-recording block per week. If that
time is unavailable, reduce the prospect count while keeping the same order of
work and decision gates; do not replace conversations with more development.

FloristApp and Floraboard sell broader storefront/operations systems. Lacy Bird
lists a cheaper self-service design tool. The proposed position is personal setup
of a narrow customer-to-WhatsApp flow, not the cheapest software, automatic
checkout, AI photorealism or a replacement for a shop's existing workflow. The
competitor's +35% order-value claim in the supplied screenshot is their claim,
not evidence for Nebula. Official pages checked 30 September 2026:
[FloristApp](https://www.floristapp.shop/en),
[Floraboard](https://floraboard.com/),
[Lacy Bird](https://bb.lacybird.com/).

## Exact offer to test

**Founding Florist Pilot:** one shop, one Nebula-hosted builder link, using the
existing Classic/Dome/Flat Heart layouts and artwork. Configure from the shop's
supplied name, logo, WhatsApp number, one currency and approved catalog prices.
Give one preview and one review round for those settings. No basic setup fee.
Charge **$29/month after the shop's version goes live**; cancellation at any time.
The customer sees an indicative estimate and prepares a WhatsApp request; the
customer must press Send and the florist confirms stock, substitutions, delivery
and final price. No automatic payment or order confirmation.

Before publishing or quoting, the founder must approve the proposed operational
cap: one batch of up to 10 price/text/contact edits and up to 20 minutes of
asynchronous help per month, without rollover. If a real capacity limit exists,
say "first three founding shops"; otherwise do not invent scarcity. Custom
domains, new photography/artwork, species/layouts, full websites, integrations,
urgent work and extra revisions need a separate written quote. Do not promise a
free 30-day trial, guaranteed sales, lifetime $29 or unlimited changes.

Get **written acceptance of the $29 scope before doing personal setup**. A
shop-specific preview is a sales step after that acceptance, not unlimited free
design work. Choose a reliable payment/invoice method and record the first billing
date before the first customer goes live. Log setup minutes, monthly support
minutes and direct hosting cost per shop; $29/month is $348/year in gross revenue
before these costs and taxes.

## Target shop and exclusion rule

Start with one segment: owner-run florists who visibly sell **custom bouquets**
through Instagram and invite customers to WhatsApp. A strong prospect has recent
custom-bouquet posts, a reachable owner, an active WhatsApp order path and a
catalog broadly compatible with the existing builder. Record the evidence URL and
date. Do not target shops that mainly need POS, delivery management, inventory,
auto-payment, a photo-realistic product catalog or a full custom website. Do not
claim to be physically local to an area unless that is true.

Notion fields: shop, profile URL, location/time zone, owner/contact channel,
custom-bouquet evidence, WhatsApp evidence, catalog fit (high/medium/low), first
contact date, message version, reply, demo viewed, exact objection, $29 scope
accepted (yes/no/unknown), next action/date, setup time, live date, first bill,
and actual requests. Keep contact data private; do not put customer phone numbers
in analytics or public notes.

## Launch gate: 30 September–2 October

The founder is updating the pricing site. Give that work **one focused day**, not
an open-ended redesign. It must say the same $29 terms as the Instagram bio and
florist landing page; show a normal flower bouquet, the estimate, and exactly
what the florist receives; distinguish demo prices from approved shop prices.
The current public site was last observed with the retired $650 + $49 offer, an
older builder and a broken `/flower` path, so verify live routes and copy after
deployment. The local `$29` page is a draft until then.

Before sending traffic, test on an actual phone: build a normal rose bouquet;
open WhatsApp; press Send **to a number the founder controls**; open the received
sheet on a second device; confirm the item list, provisional estimate and return
links. Repeat once on the target shop's device/browser before their launch.
Review the current order-sheet URL, which encodes customer contact details, before
real customer use; minimize exposed details where possible. Test the lead form
with synthetic data and verify where the enquiry lands. Resolve any broken CTA,
misleading claim, undelivered handoff, or privacy blocker before public traffic.

This is the product freeze: fix only conversion blockers, incorrect prices,
broken phone flows, buyer-confirmed catalog gaps and real order/privacy issues.
No dashboard, AI photo-generation, payments, inventory, new layout or strawberry
polish until a buyer shows it is necessary to purchase or use the product.

## Week 1: show and learn, 3–7 October

Make three assets from the current release, each clearly labeled as a demo with
sample prices: (1) a 15–20 second normal-bouquet screen recording; (2) a four-frame
carousel asking which details customers omit when requesting a custom bouquet;
(3) a 20-second handoff recording showing the real WhatsApp draft and sheet.
Existing scripts are in `marketing/pinned-post-carousel.md`. Show the shop's
approval step; do not show fake customers, fabricated outcomes or someone else's
floral photos without permission.

Research 20 shops from public business profiles and add evidence to Notion.
Reach up to five well-matched owners per day, with one short personalized message
and one useful follow-up only. Ask about their current quoting friction before
asking them to inspect a link. Offer a 25-second demo; send it when invited. If
they engage, state the real $29 scope early and ask whether they would test one
customer request. Do not send messages on the founder's behalf without explicit
authorization.

Opening to personalize:

> Hi [name], I saw your [specific custom bouquet]. When someone asks for a
> bouquet in DMs, which details do you usually have to ask for before giving a
> price? I made a short visual flow that lets them choose an idea, see an
> estimate from your prices, and send the details to WhatsApp for your approval.
> Would a 25-second demo be useful?

After interest:

> The basic setup is one shop link using the existing flower library. It's
> $29/month after your version is live, no setup fee for that scope, and you can
> cancel anytime. You confirm the final price and availability. Would that
> scope fit your shop, or is something essential missing?

One follow-up after several business days:

> Here is the exact result: choices, quantities, a sample estimate and the
> requested date in a WhatsApp message you review. Would that make your custom
> bouquet conversation clearer, or do customers already send all those details?

## Weeks 2–3: price acceptance and first paid pilot, 8–23 October

Expand to 40–50 qualified shops total across the month, based on quality and
available time. Hold five brief demo conversations or voice-note exchanges with
owners who can judge the workflow. Ask three concrete questions: (1) What detail
is missing from the request? (2) Would you use this link with one real customer?
(3) With the stated setup and limits, would you pay $29/month after it goes live?
Record their words verbatim. A polite "nice" is not price acceptance.

For an owner who accepts, collect only the required shop inputs, agree the
library/price scope in writing, time the setup, deliver one preview and one
review round, test on their phone, then agree a live date and payment method.
Only count a **paying shop** when money is collected, and only count a **real
customer request** when the shop confirms receiving one. Track what had to be
manually repaired for that first request.

Old Instagram conversations need a truthful reset. Some earlier pitches mentioned
dashboards, automated deposits or full custom stores that this $29 builder does
not have. A warm lead may be worth contacting, but correct those promises before
asking for money. Do not imply that Julia, Ashley, Atlanta or Gherzi agreed to
the current scope or price. Example:

> I need to correct my earlier description: the current $29 version doesn't
> include an order dashboard or automated deposits. It gives your customers a
> visual bouquet builder and sends their request to your WhatsApp for approval.
> Would that simpler version be useful for your shop? I can send the current
> 25-second demo.

## Week 4: decide from the funnel, 24–30 October

Targets for this **experiment**, not predicted conversion rates: 40–50 qualified
shops researched/contacted, at least 5 substantive demo conversations, at least
2 explicit yeses to the exact $29 scope, and **1 paying shop** that can receive a
real request. Publish two useful Instagram posts weekly plus stories from real
demo interactions; judge them by qualified conversations, not likes or views.

Review the funnel each Friday: qualified contacts → actual replies → demos
reviewed → price accepted → shop inputs supplied → live shops → paid shops →
real requests. Also review setup hours, support minutes, hosting cost and the
specific objection at each loss. Treat unread DMs separately from rejections.

Decision rules:

- If around 25 carefully matched contacts get almost no replies, change the
  opening, account targeting or channel. Do not add product features to solve an
  unseen-message problem.
- If at least five owners see the demo but none accepts $29, interview why and
  change the offer/segment before building more. Do not interpret polite praise
  as purchase intent.
- If two owners accept the price but cannot use the flow because of the same
  concrete blocker, fix that blocker and retest; keep the rest frozen.
- If a shop pays but setup/support consumes too much time, narrow inclusion or
  quote setup/custom work separately before scaling.
- If 30 October arrives with no payment and no explicit price acceptance after
  sufficient seen demos, pause feature and ad spending and run a fresh offer
  decision. That is a commercial pause, not evidence the founder's work had no
  value.

## Spending rule

No paid Instagram ads during the first learning cycle. The public offer, phone
handoff, and one owner willing to accept the exact price come first. If those
exist, test ads only with a predefined small budget, a demo/lead destination
and a way to attribute **qualified enquiries**, not likes. Use Notion Business
for the evidence log. Use LinkedIn Premium to identify owners, respecting each
platform's rules; do not bulk scrape or buy unverified lists. Save Lovable
credits for a repeatedly observed buyer blocker. VPN and other subscriptions
do not change buyer demand.

## The founder's next three actions

1. Approve the exact $29 service boundary and write the payment/renewal terms.
2. Finish and verify the pricing/landing/public builder update plus the real
   phone handoff and order-link privacy check.
3. Record the normal-bouquet demo and start the first 20-shop evidence list.
