## Current offer and product truth

Nebula helps independent florists who sell custom bouquets through Instagram and WhatsApp. Customers compose a visual bouquet idea, see an indicative estimate based on configured shop prices, and prepare a request for WhatsApp. The customer presses Send; the florist confirms availability, feasibility and final price.

The working offer is $29/month for one Nebula-hosted shop link, no basic setup fee, payment after the shop version goes live, cancel anytime. It uses the existing artwork/layout library and includes setup of supplied shop information and approved catalog prices, plus one review round. The existing scope caps monthly changes at one batch of up to 10 price/text/contact edits and 20 minutes of asynchronous help. Custom domains, new artwork, integrations and broader websites require separate quotes. Use the current scope in `.agents/product-marketing.md`; do not invent a new price or unlimited service promise.

The local builder now has 10 featured recipes across romance, spooky and autumn collections; editable flowers and supported extras; mobile movement controls; EN/ES; design sharing and exports. Nine collection images are AI-generated style illustrations. They are not photographs of fulfilled customer orders or an exact guarantee of the editable design.

No automatic payment/deposit collection, stock reservation, confirmed booking or shared order dashboard. No verified sales lift or time-saving percentage. Local functional checks do not prove that a real shop will pay or that the current public deployment matches local files. Record a real-phone WhatsApp test after deployment before promoting that journey as verified.

## Fourteen-day test, starting after live verification

Assumption: roughly one hour a day and two short recording blocks. Aim for one paid shop; this is a target, not a promised outcome.

| When | Work | Observable result |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Confirm the public offer, current demo, shop contact route and real-phone handoff. Record one ordinary rose bouquet from selection through WhatsApp. | One accurate demo and a working route to enquire |
| Days 2–3 | Review the three prior conversations with the clearest commercial questions. Draft an individual update explaining the narrower $29 offer. Founder reviews/sends. | Current interest, explicit decline or no response recorded separately |
| Days 3–7 | Publish the demo and problem carousel below. Build a list of 20 suitable independent florists with current evidence of custom bouquets and WhatsApp ordering. | Qualified list; two posts that explain the product |
| Days 4–10 | Send up to five individual, researched introductions on an outreach day. Share a demo when wanted. Offer an asynchronous walkthrough or short call. | Replies, demo requests and specific objections |
| Days 8–12 | One useful follow-up after several business days where appropriate; stop on rejection. Ask interested owners directly about the $29 scope. | Explicit price/scope response |
| Days 11–14 | Configure one shop only after written scope acceptance. Verify its prices, request recipient and workflow with the owner. Agree billing method/date; collect payment after go-live. | Paid pilot, or a documented reason it did not happen |

Target shops whose existing menu fits the library. Prior interest in a full website is not automatically interest in this narrower builder. Do not build unpaid bespoke sites to obtain a reply.

Spend more time on qualified conversations and follow-up than polishing content. Hold paid ads during this small test. Keep Halloween as one demonstration of a year-round product, not the only reason to buy.

Review after 20 qualified introductions or 14 days, whichever gives a useful first sample. Low response suggests reviewing audience, delivery and opening clarity; it does not prove lack of demand. If owners reply but do not try the demo, inspect trust and explanation. If five owners try it and none accept the paid scope, record their objections and revisit fit/offer before adding features. These are operational review points, not statistical proof.

## Three ready-to-produce posts

### 1. Twenty-second demo reel — introduce the product

Audience: florist owners. Use an actual screen recording, readable on a phone.

| Time | Screen | On-screen text / voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Ordinary rose bouquet in the builder | “Florists: what if a custom request started with a picture?” |
| 3–8s | Change a supported flower choice and wrapping | “Your customer starts with a bouquet idea.” |
| 8–12s | Show the estimate | “An estimate based on your configured prices.” |
| 12–17s | Open the WhatsApp draft and show its design link | “They send the details. You confirm availability and final price.” |
| 17–20s | Return to the editable bouquet | “Want to try the florist demo? DM DEMO.” |

Caption:

> A visual starting point for your next custom-bouquet conversation. Customers choose a design, see an estimate, and send their request to your WhatsApp. You stay in charge of availability and the final quote. Nebula starts at $29/month for one shop link, with no basic setup fee. DM DEMO to see the current builder.

Show “demo prices” where applicable. Do not stage a received order as a real customer sale. Respond to DEMO requests manually unless an actual messaging integration is later configured and authorized.

### 2. Five-slide carousel — preserve the florist's role

1. “Florists: what do you need before you can quote a custom bouquet?”
2. “The flowers. The colours. The size. The details.”
3. “Nebula gives customers a visual starting point.” Use a genuine editable-preview screenshot.
4. “They send the request to WhatsApp. You confirm what is possible and the final price.” Show a clearly labelled sample request.
5. “Would this fit your shop? DM DEMO to try it.”

Caption:

> Some customers know exactly what they want. Others need your guidance. I built Nebula to give that conversation a visual starting point, while keeping the final design and quote with the florist. Which detail do you usually need to clarify first?

### 3. Fifteen-second seasonal reel — demonstrate flexibility

Hook: “A spooky bouquet your customer can help customise.”
Show the mask on, remove it so flowers fill the centre, change an available extra, then show the request action. Use current working UI; do not imply every imagined toy or flower is supported.

Caption:

> One Halloween idea, several ways to customise it. Nebula lets customers explore the available options and send you a design request. You confirm the flowers, extras and final price. Built for florists taking custom orders through WhatsApp. DM DEMO for the walkthrough.

Publish this after the ordinary bouquet demo so the account clearly sells a tool for florists. Use actual product footage as proof; decorative generated imagery can support it but should not replace it.
